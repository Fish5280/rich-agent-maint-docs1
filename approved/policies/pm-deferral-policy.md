# Preventive Maintenance Deferral Policy

Policy PM-007, applies to all Skyline Plastics plants. Owner: Maintenance lead.

## 1. Purpose
Preventive maintenance (PM) protects equipment life. Sometimes production needs force a PM to be delayed.
This policy sets the limits on that.

## 2. When a deferral is allowed
- A PM may be deferred for a documented production need, such as a customer rush order.
- The deferral must be recorded in the maintenance system with the reason and the requester.
- Safety-critical PM tasks are never deferred.

## 3. Rescheduling
- Deferred preventive maintenance must be rescheduled within 21 days.
- The new date must be entered in the maintenance system, linked to the deferred record.
- If the new date also slips, the same rule applies from the new date.

## 4. Approval
- Every deferral must be approved by the maintenance lead.
- The approval is recorded in the notes of the maintenance record.
- A deferral without a recorded approval is treated as a missed PM.

## 5. Escalation
- A PM that is more than 30 days past its original date is reported to the plant manager.
- A PM that is more than one full interval overdue is reported to the plant manager and the maintenance lead.

## 6. Reporting
- The maintenance lead reviews all open and deferred PM records each month.
- Missed and overdue PM counts are reported in the monthly operations review.

## 7. Examples of valid reasons
- A customer rush order that needs all available machine hours.
- A critical spare part on backorder.
- A planned plant shutdown that will cover the work.

## 8. Roles
- Requester: the production supervisor who asks for the deferral.
- Approver: the maintenance lead.
- Owner of the new date: the planner who schedules the technician.

## 9. Records
- Deferred records stay in the system with status skipped; the replacement record carries the new date.
- Notes should name the order or event that caused the deferral.
- Records are kept for seven years.

# Troubleshooting: Motor Overheating

Applies to screw drive motors on injection molding machines. Work through the checklist in order and write
down what you find at each step.

## 1. Before you start
- Confirm the reading: compare the drive temperature with a handheld infrared reading at the frame.
- Note the motor make, model and the time the alarm first appeared.
- Do not reset a thermal trip until the motor is below 70 C.

## 2. Ambient and ventilation
- Check the zone air temperature near the motor. Compare it with the motor's rated ambient limit in its manual.
- Check that the fan cowl is clean and the fan turns freely.
- Check whether the zone cooling unit is running. Look for open facility work orders on the zone's HVAC.
- A change in ambient that lines up with the start of the overheating is a strong lead.

## 3. Lubrication history
- Look up the last completed lubrication for the motor and compare it with the manual's interval.
- Look for skipped or deferred preventive tasks, and whether they were rescheduled.
- Overdue lubrication raises bearing friction, which adds heat and can show as rpm fluctuation.

## 4. Load and duty cycle
- Compare the production schedule: extra shifts, overtime, a heavier product.
- Check the screw torque and the barrel set points for the current product.

## 5. Voltage
- Measure the three phase voltages at the motor terminals. Imbalance above 2% heats the windings.
- Check for a loose terminal or a warm contactor.

## 6. Bearing condition
- Listen for roughness; check vibration; check bearing housing temperature.
- Check rpm fluctuation in the sensor data under constant load.

## 7. Compare against identical motors
- Compare against identical motors on the same part number, in the same plant and in sister plants.
- Compare the corrective maintenance history by make and model. A pattern across plants points to the
  motor design or supplier rather than to one machine.
- If one motor differs from its siblings, look for what is different about that motor's location and history.

## 8. Reporting
- State the evidence for each cause you checked, with dates and values.
- Separate what is confirmed from what is suspected.
- Recommend next steps with an owner and a date.
